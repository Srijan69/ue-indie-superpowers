# Game Production Planning

Production is how a small team actually finishes.

## Milestones

1. **Prototype:** is the core mechanic fun in greybox?
2. **Vertical slice:** one polished section at target quality, packaged.
3. **Alpha:** all features in, rough content, playable end to end.
4. **Beta:** content complete, bug fixing, balance, performance.
5. **Release candidate:** only blockers fixed; certification/store requirements.
6. **Post-launch:** patches and community.

Each milestone has a **definition of done** you can test.

## Planning method

1. Ask for the deadline, weekly hours, team skills and budget.
2. Build a **backlog** from the design: tasks sized S/M/L; tag Must/Should/Could/Won't.
3. Plan in **1-week goals** that each produce a playable result.
4. **Estimate honestly:** multiply first guesses by 2 to 3 for new systems; include UI, audio, VFX, tuning and testing in every feature.
5. Keep **20 to 30% buffer**; track what actually took how long and adjust.
6. Prepare the **cut list** now, in order of what to cut first.
7. **Build a packaged version weekly** (catch packaging problems early) and playtest every milestone.
8. Maintain a **risk list** (technical unknowns, scope, assets, burnout) with a mitigation each.

## Weekly review

What shipped (playable)? What slipped and why? Fun check: what's the best and worst part right now? Next week's top 3 outcomes. Anything to cut?

## Overwhelmed or behind?

Shrink the next step to something finishable in a day. Cut or defer features before cutting sleep or quality of life. Remind them that a smaller finished game beats a larger unfinished one.

## Marketing timeline (light)

Hook and logline early · GIF-able moments recorded as you build · store page up early · wishlists drive launch · demo or playtest builds · devlogs and community feedback. It needs scheduled time, not leftovers.
